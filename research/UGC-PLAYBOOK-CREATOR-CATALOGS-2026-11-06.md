# UGC playbook from the creator catalogs (2026-11-06)

**For UGC product ads only (EkkoVision beater and cuff strap, Trybe brands, TikTok Shop ads). Not for OB's organic videos.**

## How this was built
- Pulled with ScrapeCreators (about 390 credits used, balance 1,812 after). Every transcript is an automatic transcript (Instagram AI transcript or TikTok caption track), so brand and platform names can be misheard ("Drive" in the Colin transcripts means Trybe). Quotes here are cut under 15 words; everything else is my paraphrase.
- **@ugcclubceo (Colin Reich, "Trybe Coach", Instagram)**: all 237 reels listed (no TikTok account found by search); 220 transcribed (every reel from 15 s to 2 min except the 4 already carded: DfSHpxDgCuo, DfZy_aNgaMc, DfFwlvYgLtl, DeZ8jUxJ6gI). Skipped: 3 reels over 2 minutes (Dc_gddIgZyv, DbOHiXpOxBV, DaNtmlyg9np) and reels under 15 s. "Hooks that always work" series: only Ep.3 (DfZy_aNgaMc, carded) and the Sep 30 "three hooks" reel (Dd7IdtvJsuW) are numbered episodes; the rest of the hook teaching lives in unnumbered reels listed below, all captured.
- Six other teachers (TikTok): **@jasen_frederique** (24 transcripts, the Trybe-specific one), **@avatiktokcoach** (18), **@thenickfowler** (13), **@ugcwithnyomi** (14 transcripts plus 3 frame sheets), **@ugc.withrach** (11 transcripts plus 4 frame sheets), **@heyimtran** (3). Plus **Nate West @watedontwait**: 12 videos not in his 42-transcript file (11 with text).
- Visual-hook videos (Rach, Nyomi) say almost nothing out loud, so I read the on-screen text from frame sheets.
- **Trybe's own Top 100 / Top Formats is not public** (an in-app tab, OB is logged in). A web search found no published Trybe format data. What exists publicly is the creators' descriptions of how they use it (section 9). To capture the real thing: OB screenshots the Top Formats panel of the Ekko-like brands he applies to.
- Rule for all numbers: every earnings, view and "this always works" statement below is the **creator's own claim**, not verified.
- Excluded on purpose: fake comments made on a comment-generator site (Colin DeMw5bMgpQp, deceptive and a disclosure risk), dressing as a nurse or other fake job (Ava), body-shaming or "if you can't bench X" openers (they break the no-viewer-body rule), and GLP-1 audience targeting (Colin DewnoTkgw0O, supplements only, SKILL.md says GLP-1 needs OB's call).

**Editing, filming, scaling, application rules (Colin, all his advice or claims)**
- Pick the audience on purpose: a 55 percent hook rate with a tiny click rate means the hook attracted the wrong people; a lower hook rate with a strong click rate is better. For a male product, talk about football or golf, not rage bait. (DfGEKuIpaNU)

## HOOK FORMAT LIBRARY (master list, deduped across creators)
Each entry: template, when to use, one example for the Ekko beater and/or cuff strap. Facts used: beater 95 percent organic cotton and 5 percent spandex, 350 gsm, short/regular/tall, hides gyno (brand claim), #1 selling beater on TikTok (claim), Black Friday sale; strap = cuff for cable kickbacks, single-arm pushdowns, pullovers and curls plus a grip strap for pulldowns, rows and RDLs, widely copied, "patent coming soon" (brand statement), men and women. First person or product truth only. OB checks every product claim against the brand page before filming.

1. **Authority + dream outcome + DQ** (Colin, carded). `[Real proof]. [Dream outcome]. But if you want [cheap version], keep scrolling.` When: any product with a proof line on screen. Beater: "Number one selling beater on TikTok (shop screen). Hides gyno. But if you want a see-through tank, keep scrolling." Strap: "Widely copied cuff, patent coming soon. If you want the knockoff, keep scrolling."
2. **Not because / but because** (Colin, carded). Three "Not because" lines then the sale. When: a real deal exists (Black Friday). Every "not because" line is a feature plus an outcome, never a bare feature (Colin, xlZuQhsaCo8). Beater: "Not because it sits high on my lats, so I'm not out here showing side boobs. Not because I can wear the same one to the gym and out to dinner. But because I paid full price last week and the sale just went live." Sale reveal only with a real, confirmed sale; otherwise a joke reveal. **Upgrade 11-10 (@bbickugc, ZPLGUvpjD):** a contrast line ("unlike [the rest of the category]"), "which is crazy given [top benefit]" after the price, a real proof number, then a variant-pick close (the pack or the size). OB's five product scripts: `UGC-LESSON-tiktok-ZPLGUvpjD-stay-away-bought-before-sale-2026-11-10.md`.
3. **Secret want, below the surface** (Colin, carded; C7, C8). Feature + "so" + private outcome. When: before writing any hook. Beater: "Short, regular and tall so I stop wearing a size up to hide my chest."
4. **If-statement identity hook** (C1). `If you're a [specific lifter type] who wants [dream outcome] without [annoying trade-off], [product].` When: audience-targeted, supplement or gear. Strap: "If you're a lifter who wants cable kickbacks without [the usual strap problem], this cuff." Keep to lifestyle, never the body. **Upgrade 11-10 (DfkOpDCg):** stack qualifiers (gender, age, occupation, lifestyle, habits) until one viewer feels named; find them in reviews, viral-video comments, the brand's long-running ads and site copy; full ladders for Beater, jeans and supplements in `UGC-LESSON-ig-DfkOpDCg-specific-if-you-are-2026-11-10.md`.
5. **Hook / primer / super hook** (C2). If-line, one sentence of context, then the promise. When: 20 to 30 s ads. Beater: hook "If you hate buying new shirts every two months", primer "I tested these for [number of weeks I really do]", super hook "I only buy this one now."
6. **Here's how to [fix] without [usual cost]** (C3b). Beater: "Here's how to wear a tank in the gym without wearing a size up." Strap: "Here's how to do single-arm pushdowns without gripping a rope."
7. **Stay away from [product]** (C3c, carded DfSHp). "Stay away from this beater, once you wear it you won't wear [old tank]." Reveal fast, reads like a bad review otherwise.
8. **Keep-scrolling anti-hook** (C4, Nate named callout). `If you [buying habit], keep scrolling.` Beater: "If you pick the cheapest tank on the shelf, keep scrolling." Strap: "If you're happy with a knockoff cuff, keep scrolling."
9. **Level 1 vs level 100** (C5). Two versions back to back. Strap: level 1 "this cuff has a strap, buy it"; level 100 a short gym-floor story about changing attachments between sets.
10. **"The ___ method"** (C6). Name a mechanism: "the single-arm cuff method". When: strap demo with one named setup.
11. **Stop doing X** (A9, A3). "Stop [habit] when [product]." Strap: "Stop wrapping a handle around your wrist for kickbacks." Product-fact check needed.
12. **Reply to a real comment** (A8, C15). Real comment on screen, reply as hook. Beater: reply to a comment about sizing, answer with short/regular/tall. Real comments only.

## EDITING AND SCALING RULES (deduped)
1. Match visual to verbal: any claim gets an overlay, a screenshot or B-roll on its word. (J15, Tran, Y9)
2. Remove dead space; overlap words slightly; keep jump cuts tight. (J16, Colin one-line-at-a-time)
3. Start every segment with movement; do not hold the product still for more than about 2 seconds. (A2)
4. Slow keyframe push-ins at each cut; punch the product forward when shown. (J15, ZPLaGnpNe card) Zoom in and out is fine on UGC (OB 11-09).
5. Safe zone: faces, captions and overlays in the middle third. (Tran)
6. Captions in one brand font, move them around the frame, light animation. (Y6; OB's rule is one brand font, so only the moving-position part is new)
7. Sound effects on beats (whoosh, pop, ping). (Y7)
8. Lighting: three lights, front brightness centred. Back camera, 4K. (J17, Y8, Nate)
9. 4K 60 export; laptop cut workflow with waveform jump-and-cut. (Nate 7630655040381930782)
10. One shoot, many cuts: one body, many hooks (library 29); or one hook shell, many products.
11. Keep 75 percent of output on proven angles, 25 percent on tests. (J18)
12. Submit 2 to 3 videos, wait for feedback, then batch. (J20)
13. Few brands, deep focus (Colin cap of 4, Jasen rotates more). Test both against OB's own approval and launch rates.
14. Real-view data beats guessing: a high hook rate with a low click rate means the wrong audience. (Colin)
15. Brand does not run an approved video: improve the video before assuming the brand is the issue. (Colin, claim)
16. Check campaign calendars (rewards races) and sales before filming. (Nate)
17. Copy the top 10 and add one swing. (Colin)
18. Application: recreate the brand's #1 ad with the real product, attach as the Trybe preview video. (Colin, Jasen)
19. Re-hook flops: swap in a static swap or a new first 5 seconds on a video that did not perform. (J7)
20. Ads are seen many times before buying; ship variants. (Colin)
