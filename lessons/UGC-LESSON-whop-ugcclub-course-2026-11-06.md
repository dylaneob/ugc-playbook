### U1. The V-Squad Script Writer GPT, and how he prompts it (lesson 12, section 4)
Colin's custom GPT ("I put my best scripts into this GPT"): `https://chatgpt.com/g/g-6a2ab5c184108191a7e756f59daf5087-v-squad-script-writer` (link read off his screen in the Discord #ai-script-writer channel; OB needs ChatGPT to open it).
Method: paste the product page link first, then **dictate** (voice) a story brief, not "write a script". Ask for teleprompter style and a max length, and tell it who to target. Then revise in a second message.
His dictated prompt, as transcribed:
```
Make me a script about how I used to be addicted to nicotine pouches. And when I first got Ultra pouches, I kind of laughed. I was like, what is in this? There's no nicotine. There's no caffeine. Like what's the point? And then I tried them and everything changed. I was absolutely locked in and I never got more done in less time. Talk about how Joe Rogan shouted them out and how they've sold out multiple times. Make it in teleprompter style so I can read it easily while I film and make it 50 seconds maximum. Really, really target my past self in this.
```
His revise message:
```
That's okay, but I want to be more controversial in the beginning, and please don't say "listen to me for 50 seconds".
```
The GPT returns five blocks: HOOK, SCRIPT (one short line per row), TEXT ON SCREEN (3 bullets), SHOT IDEAS (5 bullets), CTA. It also flagged on its own that the Rogan and sold-out lines need to be true and allowed by the brand brief.

### U3. "Stop talking like a bot": line check for every script (lesson 15, section 4)
Run each line through three questions: Does this sound like a bot? Is it a feature or a feeling? Can they feel I'm on commission?
- **No begging CTA.** Bot: "Buy this before it sells out." Real: detached, "I don't care if you buy it, these go with or without you, I'm just putting you on." Only say "sells out" if it has.
- **Fit is a feature, the reaction is the want.** Bot: "Nicest tank ever, it fits so well." His good line is a "you" claim, so OB's version is first person: "Wore it to the gym once and got asked where it's from" (only if it happened).
- **Fabric facts become a lived comparison.** Bot: "It's comfortable because of the cotton." Real: "This thing is so soft I'd use it as a pillowcase."
- **Use your own slang** and the words you use with friends.
Strap: Bot "Great grip, durable strap." Real: "Last set of pulldowns my hands used to quit before my back did. Not with this."

### U4. The $25K video, dissected (lesson 16, section 4; a HAUS tank ad, Colin's claim: $25.1k lifetime, $370 in the last 7 days)
On-screen Trybe metrics: **47.2% hook rate, 0.91% link CTR, 21.6 s average watch (44%) on a 48 s video, 1,061 link clicks**. Use these as a reference bar for OB's own Trybe analytics.
Beat order, which he says he "tries to recreate with everything":
1. Pop-culture green-screen hook while the trend is hot (Stranger Things, "this dude has so much aura") into the secret want (people turning their heads).
2. **Show the problem on camera**, not just say it: the bad tank on his body, low on the lats. His rule: problem, dream outcome, problem, solution; never dream outcome straight to solution.
3. Solution: the good tank, "high on the lat, big difference".
4. **Multi-use**: same item worn several ways (under an open shirt, out at night). He says it makes the item feel pricier.
5. Scarcity and offer with a size reference, then the detached close: "these will be gone with or without you, I'm just putting you on".
6. Jokes throughout: "puts their guard down".
Beater version: aura-style trend hook only if a trend is live that week → OB in a thin tank you can see through (the problem, shown) → Ekko beater, 350 gsm, short/regular/tall (brand claim: hides gyno) → gym, under an open shirt, out to dinner → Black Friday price, "#1 selling beater on TikTok" (brand claim), OB's real height, weight and size → detached close. HAUS is EkkoVision's competitor: copy the structure only, never the brand.
