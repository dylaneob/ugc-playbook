## The method in my words
1. Formula: **Authority + promise a dream outcome + DQ** (DQ = disqualify). One breath, three clauses.
2. Authority: a proof statement that earns the next 10 seconds (his numbers on screen, a result, a credential).
3. Promise: the dream outcome, stated plainly ("this hook always works", "I'll show you how I finally got clear skin").
4. DQ: tell a specific type of viewer to leave ("keep scrolling", "this won't matter", "this is not for you"). Aim it at the wrong-fit buyer: flat-rate creators, overnight-result seekers, magic-pill seekers.
5. Why it works, his reasoning: the DQ adds credibility and makes the speaker look like they are not just selling, like they do not need everyone to buy.
6. Two product-side examples in the reel: acne ("after struggling with acne for six years...but if you're expecting overnight results, keep scrolling") and testosterone ("my testosterone is over 800 naturally...but if you're looking for a magic pill, keep scrolling").
7. Two musts: add visuals that prove the authority line (blood work on screen, before and after photos), and only use an authority you really have. His words: if you have no proof, "you have a bigger issue".
8. Variation logic: swap the authority, the dream outcome, the DQ target. Same skeleton every time.

Short quotes (his): "promise a dream outcome"; "keep scrolling, this won't matter".

## SCRIPT TEMPLATE (fill in the blanks)
1. **AUTHORITY (0 to 3 s, proof on screen):** "[Real proof: number, rank, result, fact about the product]."
2. **PROMISE (3 to 6 s):** "[Dream outcome], and I'll show you how." or "This is why [product] [result]."
3. **DQ (2 to 3 s):** "But if you want [wrong-fit thing: shortcut, cheap version, magic pill], keep scrolling."
4. **PROOF BEAT (5 to 10 s):** demo or on-screen evidence matching the authority line.
5. **OFFER + CTA (3 to 5 s):** "[Sale / deal]. Link is below."
Length 15 to 30 s. First clause must land in 9 words or 3 s (OB rule).

**OB rules layered on top:** authority only from the brand's own facts or OB's real, shown proof. The DQ targets a preference or buying mindset (thin shirts, knockoffs, magic pills), never the viewer's body. No "you" claims about the viewer's body. No invented stats. "Patent coming soon", never "patented". No em dashes.

## NEW vs OB's method
OB's method (ugc skill sections 4 and 5) already has: hook in 9 words or 3 s, product by 2 s, one reason to buy, offer, CTA, hook as the unit of variation, and the 11-03 "not because / but because" shape.
**New from this reel:**
- A second, structurally different hook shape: proof first, then promise, then a disqualifier. The 11-03 hook starts with a fake negative; this one starts with credibility.
- The DQ clause as a deliberate tool: telling the wrong buyer to leave reads as confidence and filters comments and returns.
- Rule that the authority line needs on-screen proof in the same seconds (his example: blood work, transformation photos).
- Variation axis separate from the first frame: the DQ target (cheap-version buyer, shortcut buyer, knockoff buyer).
- Caution: for gear with no personal-result story, authority must come from the product (sales rank, spec, copied-by-others), not an invented personal claim.

## WORKED EXAMPLE 1: EkkoVision beater (Black Friday sale)
Facts used: 95% organic cotton + 5% spandex, 350 gsm, short/regular/tall, hides gyno, #1 selling beater on TikTok, Black Friday sale.

> **AUTHORITY:** "This is the number one selling beater on TikTok."
> **PROMISE:** "350 gsm, 95% organic cotton and 5% spandex, in short, regular and tall, so it fits and nothing shows through. I stopped thinking about my chest the day I put it on."
> **DQ:** "But if you want a thin shirt that fits like a sack, keep scrolling."
> *(proof beat: fabric held up to light, stretch test, short/regular/tall side by side)*
> **OFFER + CTA:** "It's on Black Friday sale now. Link's below."

Notes: "hides gyno" appears only as first-person ("stopped thinking about my chest"); OB must confirm that line is true for him. Confirm which SKUs are 350 gsm and the sale end date before filming. The #1 claim needs the TikTok Shop rank screenshot on screen.

## WORKED EXAMPLE 2: Ekko cuff strap (no sale)
Facts used: cuff for cable kickbacks, single-arm pushdowns, pullovers, curls; grip strap for pulldowns, rows, RDLs; widely copied, "patent coming soon"; men and women.

> **AUTHORITY:** "This cuff has been copied by half the gym brands, patent coming soon."
> **PROMISE:** "Cable kickbacks, single-arm pushdowns, pullovers and curls on the cuff. Pulldowns, rows and RDLs on the grip strap. Men and women both use it."
> **DQ:** "But if you want the knockoff, keep scrolling."
> *(proof beat: kickback, then pulldown with the grip strap, copies shown only if OB has permission and no brand is named)*
> **CTA:** "Get the original, link below."

Notes: "widely copied" is safe; "patent coming soon" only until the owner confirms filing status. Do not name competitor brands on screen.

## Variation sheet (for the film sheet)
- Authority: sales rank, spec number, "widely copied", a real personal result with proof on screen.
- DQ targets: "if you want a thin shirt", "if you want the knockoff", "if you're after a shortcut", "if you only buy on sale" (pair with the sale).
- Same take, swap the DQ clause and first frame.
- Use one variation per product per batch (OB 11-03 rule applies to this shape too).
