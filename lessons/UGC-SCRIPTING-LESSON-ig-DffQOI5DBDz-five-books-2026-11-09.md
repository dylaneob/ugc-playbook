## What he says
"AI slop" comes from AI writing with no copy principles. Give it five, one per book:
1. **Breakthrough Advertising, Eugene Schwartz:** levels of awareness, so the opening matches what the prospect already knows.
2. **Great Leads, Michael Masterson (with John Forde):** six proven ways to open, so every ad doesn't open the same way.
3. **Cashvertising, Drew Eric Whitman:** the Life Force 8, eight things people are wired to want. Tie the product to one, every time.
4. **Scientific Advertising, Claude Hopkins:** specific beats vague. "Ships in two days" sells, "fast shipping" doesn't.
5. **The Robert Collier Letter Book:** enter the conversation already happening in the prospect's mind. Don't create desire, channel the desire they have onto the product.

## Checked 11-09 (secondary sources only, none of the books were opened)
- Five awareness levels: unaware, problem aware, solution aware, product aware, most aware. https://personalmba.com/levels-of-awareness/
- Six leads: offer, promise, problem-solution, secret, proclamation, story. One idea per lead ("Rule of One"). https://writewithai.substack.com/p/great-leads-kickstart-part-1-the and https://www.goodreads.com/work/quotes/19201094 . The source for the six is thin on the last four; the direct-to-indirect order below is my reading of the book, so treat it as a working rule.
- Life Force 8 (paraphrase): 1 survive, enjoy life, live longer; 2 food and drink; 3 freedom from fear, pain, danger; 4 intimate companionship; 5 comfortable living conditions; 6 status, winning, keeping up with others; 7 care for loved ones; 8 approval from others. https://www.phoneburner.com/blog/how-to-apply-the-life-force-8-for-better-selling . "Biologically wired" is Whitman's framing, not established science.
- **One correction to the reel:** "you can't create desire, you channel it" is generally credited to Schwartz, not Collier. Collier's line is the one about entering the conversation. Exact wording of both not confirmed against the books. https://taylorpearson.me/bookreview/breakthrough-advertising/

## How it plugs into /ugc
Run it on every script. It sits before the HOOK FORMAT LIBRARY pick: the library says what the ad looks like, this says what the first line is allowed to assume and what it sells to.

**Three tags at the top of the film sheet**
| Tag | Pick one | What it decides |
|---|---|---|
| Awareness | unaware, problem, solution, product, most | What the first line can take for granted. Cold Meta and TikTok are usually problem or solution aware. Retargeting and the brand's own fans are product or most aware. |
| Lead | offer, promise, problem-solution, secret, proclamation, story | How it opens. The more aware the buyer, the more direct the lead: offer and promise for people who know it, problem-solution and secret in the middle, proclamation and story for people who don't know they have the problem. One idea per lead. |
| Want | one of the Life Force 8 | What it really sells to. Beaters and jeans: mostly 6 (status), 8 (approval), 4 (companionship). Supplements: mostly 1 and 3. This feeds the secret-want step, which then makes it private and specific. |

**Two line tests**
- **Overheard (Collier):** could this first line be overheard from the buyer? Take it from real buyer language (OB's buyer-language file (not included; build your own from comments and reviews), comments, reviews) or OB's own words. If only a marketer would say it, rewrite it.
- **Specific (Hopkins):** every adjective becomes a number, a spec or something shown. "Hundreds of washes, two years" not "durable". "5'11, 215, XL" not "runs big". "26-inch quads" not "big legs". True ones only: the brand's page, the label, or what OB really did. Specific and invented is worse than vague.

**Batch rule:** one product's batch covers at least 3 lead types and at least 2 awareness levels. No two cards share the same awareness and lead pair unless they are hook swaps of one body.

## Format worth keeping
"N things your [X] needs": a row of N covers or products along the top, each lit as he names it, the current one held over his chest. Easy to build, and the tracker gives people a reason to stay for all N.
