# UGC edit lesson: roast of a Ninja waffle maker UGC video (TikTok)

- Source: https://www.tiktok.com/t/ZPLaGnpNe/ (6:00, watched 2026-11-02 with /watch, balanced, local whisper.cpp transcript, 80 frames)
- Creator: jasen_frederique (TikTok). Format: he stands in frame, screen-records a viewer's UGC video in the phone editor (TikTok/CapCut style), and re-edits it live while roasting it. Sent by OB as "a great way to learn how to edit for UGC, for variations and upgrades".
- Honest scope note: this is a single-video upgrade critique of a product demo (Ninja waffle maker). It does NOT teach hook/body/CTA variation systems, testing or scaling, and gives no performance numbers. The value is "what to fix in a UGC demo edit".

## Summary (own words)
He takes a decent demo video of a waffle maker and lists what is missing: no text hook at the start, a talking head that sits still, product features that are talked about but not shown, a dial and settings label too small to read, dead pauses, and a weak finished-product shot. His fixes are all small edit moves, not reshoots, plus a few reshoot notes (show the real waffle first, plate it with syrup). Overall verdict: a solid video, held back by missing on-screen text, no motion in the talking head, and not enough visual proof for each spoken claim.

## Lessons (with approximate timestamps)
1. Text hook on frame one (0:08-0:26). Add a short on-screen line in the first seconds; it carries the video for sound-off viewers. Even a basic line is better than none.
2. Open on the payoff shot (0:57-1:12). Start with the finished product looking great (the waffle itself, "do you see this"), then show the how-to steps. Product result first, process second.
3. Keyframe zooms on talking head (0:33-0:50, 1:19-1:40, 3:56-4:12, 4:40-4:53). A still head is boring. Cut, then keyframe a slow zoom in, and a zoom out when the pose moves forward; add a split plus keyframe on every long still shot. Keep it subtle. Slow zoom keeps people watching longer (his claim).
4. Every claim gets a visual (1:53-2:15, 3:00-3:30). "Non-stick": show the waffle sliding off the plate at that exact word. "Dial": physically twist the dial on camera while saying it. "Golden every time": close-up of the result on the word.
5. Make the thing readable (2:35-2:52). If a label or setting is unreadable at phone size, hold it to the lens or zoom in on it. The customer must see what you are talking about at all times.
6. One focal point per frame (5:13-5:23). Too much visible (waffle, product, room, you) dilutes it. Whatever is being talked about should be the focal point.
7. Cut dead space (2:19-2:31). Trim pauses; "no dead space, everything flows fast".
8. Finish with a real end-state shot (4:16-4:27). Plate the product result (syrup on top) so the final shot looks like the thing the buyer wants. Then the CTA line (price, "check the link").
9. Order of beats should follow the logic of use (1:53-2:00). Feature mentions land where they are shown (non-stick comes when the waffle comes off, not before).
10. Sound: he mentions adding small quick sound effects on actions (dial) at about 3:16 (minor, little detail given).

## NEW vs OB's method
OB already has: zero dead space cuts, b-roll matching the words, last take only, caption font, hooks as the unit of variation, product on screen by 2 s. Not new: dead space (lesson 7), words-match-visuals in general (4).
New or sharper:
- Slow keyframe punch-ins/outs on the talking head, with a cut at each push, as the standard motion layer (not in sections 4 to 6).
- Open on the payoff/result shot, then show the process (his hook variation = "result first"); OB's hook list has product in hand, reaction, problem, price, comparison, test, but not "finished result".
- Demonstrate the physical feature on the word (twist the dial, slide the item off), and hold labels/small text to the lens or zoom so they are readable on a phone.
- End-state shot before CTA (the product used and looking finished).
- Single-focal-point check on every frame (clear the background and extra items).
- Text hook on frame one for sound-off viewers is partly covered by captions rules, but "a separate short text hook line over the first 3 s" is worth testing as its own hook variable.

## How to apply to Ekko beater/strap ads
- Hook variants (cheap, no reshoot): same body, add a different text hook line on frame one; add a "result first" hook that opens on the best pump/finished-lift shot or the fit on the body before the explanation.
- Talking head/voiceover takes: after every cut, push in 3 to 6% and ease back out; never leave a still frame longer than about 2 s. Do it with the ekko.py render (scale/crop keyframes) so face tracking stays centered.
- Proof on the word: strap, "grip" or "no slip" = show the strap locking onto the bar while he says it; "fits tight" = pull on it close up; beater "coverage in all the right places" = already b-roll on the chest, keep it. Hold the label, stitching or size tag to the lens if it is mentioned.
- Close-ups: any product detail named in the script gets one macro shot at readable size; kill other items in the frame (bag, bottle, background clutter).
- End on the product in use (finished rep or finished fit check), then the deal/CTA line, with the deal background rules unchanged (product grid, slashed original price, no code).
- Test as separate variables (no data yet): text hook on/off, result-first opener vs problem opener, zoom keyframes vs none. Label results as untested until launch data exists.
