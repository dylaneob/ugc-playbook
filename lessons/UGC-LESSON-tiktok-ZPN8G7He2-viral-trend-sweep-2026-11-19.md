## The six new formats
Each one is a first line and a first frame. The body, proof and close still come from the product: its long-running ads (ad id on the film sheet), the brand's page or OB's real experience. The examples use facts already on file (the fact list in `UGC-LESSON-tiktok-ZPLGUvpjD-stay-away-bought-before-sale-2026-11-10.md` and the library's fact line). They are ungraded fill-in examples, not film sheets. Square brackets = check before filming. Anything past the first line in a template (the tier walk, the pack offer, the third beat, the reveal) is not in his video; its source is named next to it.

**#47 Tier-list ranking.** `Ranking the top [number] [product type].` An empty tier list (S to D) sits over his head from the first frame and the items are in his hands. His demo stops at the line. The body is the tier-list format in the Trybe library (format 11): walk the tiers with labels, close on the top pick.
- For OB (limits from the skill's rules, not from the video): rank his own things or generic types, never a named competitor. Every tier call is his real experience, and the top spot gets one shown reason.
- Beater: "Ranking every tank top in my drawer." [Only tanks he really has.] His old tanks that lost their shape after one wash and went see-through go low (C4448). The Beater goes top: 2+ years and hundreds of washes (C4446 to C4448).

**#48 Count-up stack.** `Not just getting one. Not just getting two. Not just getting three. [Number] of these [product] for [the real offer].` One item swings into frame and stacks on each count.
- This is an offer hook. It needs a real pack or multi-buy deal, and the count ends on the number in the deal. All three long-running ads land the pack offer in the same sentence.
- Beater: "Not just getting one. Not just getting two. Not just getting three. The three-pack of Beaters is on sale." [The 3-pack on sale is from the hero card; it needs Ekko's yes on the day.]

**#49 Two-in-one split.** Two of him in one frame. Left: `I use [single-job thing A].` Right: `I use [single-job thing B].` His demo stops there. My read, not shown in the video: the product is the one thing that does both jobs, and a third beat says so.
- Mechanics are library #22 and J5 (locked phone, mask, blended seam). Settled poses only, no setup moments (OB's twin-split rule).
- Only for a product that really replaces two things, and both "I use" lines are things OB has really used.
- Strap: left "I use a cuff for kickbacks", right "I use straps for rows", then one of him with the Ekko strap: "I use one for both." [Check the product page for both uses, and that both lines are true for OB.]
- Beater: left "I wear a gym tank", right "I wear a going-out shirt", then "Same shirt." He wears it to the gym, to the store and dressed up (C4448).

**#50 The guy who owns the place.** `If you go to [place] and you want to [dress / smell] like the guy who owns it, [product].` A status picture in one line: a place the buyer goes, and the person with the most standing there.
- Same family as #4, but a place and a role instead of an identity and a trade-off. Keep the verb on the garment or the scent (dress, wear, smell), never on the viewer's body.
- Jeans: "If you go out to dinner and you want to be dressed like the guy who owns the place, these are the jeans."
- Beater: "If you walk into the gym and you want to be dressed like the guy who owns it, wear this."

**#51 You just got scammed.** `If you bought the viral [product], you just got scammed.` Drop "viral" where it isn't true for the product. His demo stops at the line. For OB the true reason comes in the next breath (the reveal-fast rule of #2 and #7).
- Nothing on file for this one, and it reads as a bad review of the brand, so the reveal comes right away, like #2 and #7.
- The reveal is the offer (he paid full price before the sale) or an obvious joke. "Scam" is never aimed at a named competitor or seller.
- The brand reviews it, so film a clean take too ("you overpaid").
- Beater: "If you bought the Beater at full price, you just got scammed. So did I. It's about half off right now." [OB paid full price for his. The sale line needs Ekko's yes on the day.]

**#52 Like this, something like this.** `If you want to [dress / smell] like this, [wear / use] something like this.` A picture pops up in a top corner on the first "this" and he points at it. The product is pushed to the lens on the second.
- The picture is the dream outcome and the product is the answer. The product shows up last: 1.5 s in on his demo (the cut at 0:29.7), which is inside the skill's product-by-2-s rule, so the first beat stays short.
- The picture follows OB's celebrity rule: a sharp style reference, never shown as if the person wears or endorses the product. It shows a style, a fit or the product in use, never a body result, and never a result for a supplement.
- Beater: picture = a male celebrity in a fitted tank [style reference]. "If you want a tank to sit like this, wear something like this."

## Two new wordings for formats already on file
- **#7 Stay away from:** `Do not buy the viral [product].` The product is held out in one hand with a tool raised over it in the other (his is a cleaver). It is a first frame, not a product test, so nothing has to be destroyed. Say "viral" only where it is true for the product [OB's call; the Beater has 220.1K sold on the 11-09 TikTok Shop screenshot]. Reveal fast, as #7 says.
- **#2 Not because / but because:** a softer version of the skill's "I'm returning [product]" opener, `Unfortunately, I'm going to have to return this [product].` Tight close-up, product tilted at the lens, then the three not-because lines and the reveal as carded.

## How to use it
- **The sweep:** one product, one set, the same body and close, and all eight first lines filmed back to back with a new camera distance on each. That gives eight of the 8 to 12 hooks a script needs (skill section 5). Each is a different library format, so the one-variation-per-format rule holds. Hooks 4 and 7 are that batch's #7 and #2 for the product, so they take the place of its other #7 and #2 openers, they are not extra.
- **Shell from the feed, substance from the brand.** The format is borrowed. The skill's self-audit line is unchanged: what each sweep hook says (its angle) starts from the brand's long-running ads, with the ad id and days live on the film sheet, and every claim comes from the brand's page or OB's real experience. The examples above are fill-ins from the fact list only, so they are not film-ready until they carry that ad id.
- **Hook length:** the skill's rule is 9 words or 3 s, with 9 as a target. He says every one in under 3 s. OB's fills of #51 and #52 run 12 and 13 words. Two are long by design, like #46 (labelled so in the playbook) and #43 (labelled so in its card): #48 (the count is 12 words and the pack offer makes it 19) and #50 (20 to 23 words).
- **Skip a shell the product can't make true:** #48 needs a real pack deal, #49 a real two-in-one, #51 a real price drop or a joke the brand will pass, and "viral" has to be true.
- **What stands behind each shell:** #48's count-up line opens two long-running ads, and a shorter variant opens a third. #7, #50 and #52 each have a close relative that ran long, not the same line. #47 has Trybe Top 100 earnings only. #49 has no ad with its line, but five split or side-by-side compare ads ran 46 to 160 days, so the layout is on file and the two-in-one payoff is not. #51 has nothing behind it yet.
- **His speed** is one-shot takes plus an editor. OB's edit is already covered, so the cost is filming: each hook is one line, 2 to 3 s.
- **The eight are today's examples.** His method is to keep adding. A format OB keeps seeing in his feed or in a brand's Top Formats gets carded and numbered the same way.
