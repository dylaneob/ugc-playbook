## What he says (summary, slides 2 to 13)
1. **A video hook has three parts:** copy (the words said and shown on screen, "roughly 60%"), the scroll stopper (the visual behind the copy, 35%), and audio (5 to 10%; most people watch with sound off). Spend most of the time on copy and visuals.
2. **Copy works through three levers, and the best hooks stack all three:**
   - **Contrast:** something unexpected enough that scrolling past feels like a loss.
   - **Open loop:** a thought started that the viewer now needs to finish.
   - **Micro commitment:** the viewer is made to agree, react or look closer before they notice.
3. **Ad 1 (a device, woman to camera):** opens "can you see it?", then her reactions ("i think so... oh yeah... oh wow"). On-screen text curses and names a made-up sensation instead of the product ("this device"). She never shows clearly what is happening. His read: a micro commitment (she asks you to look), then several open loops.
4. **Ad 2 (a home skin device):** "look at that. that's freaky." while using it, a 2-second micro hook, then a stacked qualifier, "ladies, if you are over 40 and [an age sign on the face], then you need to listen up". He calls it Gary Halbert's if-then. She keeps using the product on camera the whole time.
5. **Ad 3 (an under-eye device):** "look what this [bleeped] under-eye device did to my face", over an uncomfortable scan image. His read: the "car crash" scroll stopper, the same micro commitment and open loop, and the bleep pulls more attention than the word would.
6. **His formula:** all three open with some version of "look at this": micro commitment, open loop, visual contrast. "Hook = copy (60%) + scroll stopper (35%) + audio (5-10%). Create contrast in everything."

## What is new for OB
1. **A 3-part hook check:** copy, scroll stopper and audio as separate lines on every film-sheet hook, with the visual written as carefully as the words. The skill already asks for the product on screen by 2 s; this adds the question "what is the scroll stopper behind the words?"
2. **A 3-lever check on each hook:** contrast, open loop, micro commitment. A hook that has none gets rewritten; the best have all three.
3. **Two new library formats, #53 and #54** (playbook):
   - **#53 Look-closer opener:** a 1 to 3 word question or command that makes the viewer look ("Can you see it?", "Look at that."), said while the product is being used on camera, before any claim.
   - **#54 Micro hook + qualifier stack:** #53 first (about 2 s), then an if-you qualifier (#43 to #46 style) while the product stays in use on screen.
4. **Bleeped curse (optional):** a mild curse in the first line, bleeped in the edit. Curse openers ran long in 9 of 384 ads (all unbleeped); the bleep is untested.

## Limits for OB
- Nothing about the viewer's body or face, and no result claims about OB's own face or body from a product. His ad 2's qualifier ("over 40", an age sign on the face) is the shape, not the content: OB's qualifier stays on the garment, the buying habit or the situation, as #43 to #46 already say.
- No made-up "brain orgasm" style claims of a sensation or result. Contrast comes from a true, unexpected line.
- "Look at that" has to point at something true and visible: the spandex snapping back, the tank sitting high on the chest, the jeans in a squat.
- A curse is OB's call per brand. His 11-09 Ekko takes already include a gas / ass split (C4444 to C4452); the bleep is optional.
- "Profitable", "the formula" and the percentages are his claims only.

## How to use it
- Every film-sheet hook gets three short lines: Copy / Scroll stopper / Audio, and a tag for which levers it uses (contrast, open loop, micro commitment).
- #53 and #54 are one variation per product per batch, like the other library formats. Test them; they are not proven in OB's apparel set.
