# UGC lesson: name the person and the failure, then "do them a favor" (2026-11-09)

Source: @alexdoesugc, TikTok, 82 s, https://www.tiktok.com/t/ZPLbn9jRH/ (sent by OB 11-09). Watched: full transcript and 10 frames. His earnings ($795 that day, a little under $10K in the past month) are his claims only.

## What the video is

A Trybe tips video, not an ad. One selfie take in a bedroom, backward hat, wired earbud mic held up to his mouth. White word-by-word captions in the middle, a fixed headline box up top for each tip ("1. Call out people's insecurities/painpoints", "3. Don't pitch them. Do them a favor"). He swings the product tub into frame on the word when he gives an example (0:20, 1:04).

## The three tips

1. **Call out the pain and the insecurity. Don't just praise the product.** Nobody feels seen by "it gives me so much energy." His format: "If you're an easily distracted desk worker who actually wants to finish what you start, but you reread the same paragraph three times and lose your train of thought halfway through," and only then intro the product. Up to that point nothing has been sold.
2. **Volume, without giving up quality.** He calls out people submitting static ads to brands.
3. **Don't pitch, do them a favor.** Not "this stuff is so good, buy it today." Instead: "So if any of this sounds familiar, I'll do you a favor and I'll leave the link below for you." His reason: the click feels like their decision.

## Reusable

**Library #43, Person + want + private failure.** `If you're [specific person] who wants [outcome], but [a specific small moment of failing], [product].` Sharper than #4, because the "but" clause is a scene, not a trade-off.

**Library #44, The favor close.** `If any of this sounds familiar, I'll do you a favor and leave the link below.` Same family as "I'm just putting you on."

**Visual:** swing the product into frame on the word that names it. A mic held in hand works as a look for a talking-head variation.

## Limits for OB's ads

- The failure has to be about the garment, the gear or a situation, never the viewer's body.
- Long hook by design, about 20 words. It breaks the 9-word target, so treat it as one variation per product per batch.
- Tip 2's point on statics is his opinion. Six of the 214 reference ads were static or slideshow.
